Project phases—and why they matter

01 · Discovery & kick-off (Weeks 1–2)

What we do

Confirm the project purpose, stakeholders, current workflow, objectives, constraints, and baseline measures.

Why it matters

Starting with a shared understanding helps avoid solving the wrong problem and establishes realistic expectations before commitments are made.

Outputs

Project charter, stakeholder map, current-state workflow, initial risks, and baseline measures.

02 · Requirements & workflow design (Weeks 3–5)

What we do

Gather user and operational requirements, map the future workflow, prioritise needs, and agree scope.

Why it matters

Clinicians and administrative users need a workflow that reflects how care is actually delivered. Early alignment reduces late changes and confusion.

Outputs

Requirements catalogue, future-state workflow, prioritised scope, and agreed acceptance criteria.

03 · Configuration & preparation (Weeks 6–9)

What we do

Coordinate configuration activities, clarify dependencies, track decisions, and review progress with technical and operational teams.

Why it matters

Visible dependencies and timely decisions help prevent small blockers from becoming schedule-critical issues.

Outputs

Configuration progress, decision log, dependency tracker, updated project plan, and issue list.

04 · Testing & user acceptance (Weeks 10–11)

What we do

Prepare test scenarios, coordinate user acceptance testing, record defects, and confirm that agreed requirements are met.

Why it matters

Testing with users helps identify workflow problems before they affect live operations and gives stakeholders evidence for readiness decisions.

Outputs

Test scenarios, defect log, UAT results, acceptance decisions, and outstanding actions.

05 · Training & readiness (Weeks 12–13)

What we do

Coordinate training, communications, user support arrangements, readiness checks, and contingency planning.

Why it matters

A technically ready solution can still fail if people do not understand the new process or know where to get support.

Outputs

Training materials, attendance tracking, communication plan, readiness checklist, and support plan.

06 · Pilot & go-live (Weeks 14–15)

What we do

Start with a defined pilot area, monitor issues and feedback, review agreed go-live criteria, and support a formal Go/No-Go decision.

Why it matters

A controlled pilot allows the team to learn and respond before broader rollout. A readiness gate makes the decision transparent.

Outputs

Pilot feedback, go-live decision, issue tracker, contingency actions, and rollout recommendation.

07 · Hypercare & evaluation (Week 16)

What we do

Provide enhanced early support, triage issues, review user feedback and performance measures, and agree handover into ongoing support.

Why it matters

Early adoption is a critical period. Monitoring helps stabilise the workflow and identify improvements while the experience is fresh.

Outputs

Hypercare log, KPI review, lessons learned, support handover, and improvement backlog.

How I would monitor delivery

Illustrative indicators could include milestone progress, open risks and issues, testing defects, training completion, adoption, user feedback, workflow time, documentation completeness, and support-ticket trends. Targets would be agreed after establishing a baseline.
